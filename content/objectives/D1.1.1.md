## Azure first

- A **Microsoft Entra role** (Global Administrator, Security Administrator…) is a job title *inside the directory*. It controls users, apps and tenant settings. Its scope is the whole tenant.
- An **Azure role** (Owner, Contributor, Key Vault Administrator…) is an Azure RBAC role. It controls *resources*. Its scope is a management group, subscription, resource group or single resource, and it inherits downward.
- A **role-assignable group** is an Entra group created with a flag that lets you assign Entra roles to it. Only highly privileged admins can change who is in it.
- **Microsoft Entra ID P2** (or **Microsoft Entra ID Governance**) is the licence that unlocks PIM. With an Azure-only lab you have neither, so this page and its screenshots are your lab.

New to these? Read the Foundations pages listed in "Before you start".

## In one sentence

Privileged Identity Management (PIM) turns a permanent admin role into one you *switch on* for a few hours — after MFA, a reason and (optionally) a colleague's approval — and then it switches itself off.

## Why it exists

As an analyst you have seen the incident where a forgotten Global Administrator account gets phished. The account had held the role for 18 months. The attacker's window was those same 18 months. That permanent, always-on role is called **standing privilege**. The damage a stolen identity can do is its **blast radius**.

PIM fixes the window, not the password. Think of a hotel key card that only opens the server room between 14:00 and 16:00 today, and only after reception checked your ID. Outside that window the card is useless — even to a thief. That model is **just-in-time (JIT) access**: you do not hold the access by default, you activate it on purpose, and it expires on its own.

Two separate things are configured in PIM. Keep them apart in your head:

- **Assignments** — *who* holds the role, as **eligible** or **active**, permanent or time-bound.
- **Role settings** (also called PIM policies) — *what an eligible person must do* to activate. Set once per role; every assignment of that role follows them.

![Mindmap of PIM split into role settings and assignments](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/wwl-sci/implement-configure-privileged-identity-management/media/privileged-entra-roles-operations.png){Training module: role settings (MFA, justification, approval, duration) are separate from assignments (eligible or active)}

## What actually matters

**1. Eligible vs active, permanent vs time-bound.** These are two independent axes.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ==@Eligible== | Holds the right to activate. No permissions until activation. |
| ==@Active== | Permissions are live now. No activation step. |
| Permanent eligible / active | No end date. |
| Time-bound eligible / active | Only between a start and end date. |
| Activated (state) | An eligible user who completed activation and is now active for a set period. |

An eligible role gives ==!exactly the same permissions== as a permanent one once activated. The difference is *when* they are live, not *what* they allow. Use active assignments only where activation is impractical, such as break-glass accounts.

**2. Role settings — the activation gate.** Per role, you choose:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| Activation maximum duration | ==@1 to 24 hours== (default 8). The user picks a duration up to this cap. The Microsoft Graph PIM API overview still says activation is "always max 8 hours"; the PIM role-settings page and portal allow up to 24 — the product UI wins. |
| On activation, require MFA | Prompts for MFA. Users who already did strong auth this session may not be prompted again. |
| On activation, require Microsoft Entra Conditional Access authentication context | Hands the activation to a Conditional Access policy — for example "phishing-resistant MFA from a compliant device". See D1.1.2. |
| Require justification | Free-text reason, written to the audit log. |
| Require ticket information | A ticket number field. Information only — ==!not checked== against any ticketing system. |
| Require approval to activate | Named approvers must approve. The approver ==@needs no admin role==. If you name nobody, active Privileged Role Administrators / Global Administrators become the approvers. |
| Assignment duration | Allow permanent eligible/active, or force them to expire after a set time. |
| Require MFA / justification on active assignment | Applies to the *admin creating* an active assignment — PIM can't challenge the user later because they are already active. |
| Notifications | Who gets emailed on assignment, activation and approval. "Critical emails only" is an option. |

The training module recommends tiers: Critical roles (Global Administrator, Privileged Role Administrator) get MFA + justification + approval and a 1–2 hour cap; High roles (Security Administrator, Privileged Authentication Administrator) 4–8 hours, approval recommended; Moderate roles (Exchange Administrator, Application Administrator) 8 hours. These are recommendations, not defaults.

**3. Three surfaces, three scopes.**

| | Microsoft Entra roles | Azure resources | PIM for Groups |
|---|---|---|---|
| What becomes eligible | A directory role | An Azure RBAC role at a scope | Group **Member** or **Owner** |
| Scope | Tenant | Management group, subscription, resource group, resource | The group (and whatever the group has access to) |
| Role settings are set | Per role | ==@Per role per scope== — Contributor on prod can be stricter than Contributor on dev | Per group: one policy for membership, one for ownership |
| Who manages it | ==@Privileged Role Administrator== (or Global Administrator) | ==@Owner== or ==@User Access Administrator== on that resource | Group owner, Privileged Role Administrator or Global Administrator |

Security Administrator and Security Reader can *view* Entra role assignments in PIM but ==!can't see Azure resource assignments by default==.

**4. PIM for Groups.** Instead of 20 people each eligible for Key Vault Contributor, give the role to one group and make the 20 people *eligible members*. Activation adds them to the group; expiry removes them. One policy, one audit trail, no drift. The feature was called "Privileged Access Groups" until January 2023.

- Any security group or Microsoft 365 group can be enabled — ==!except dynamic groups and groups synced from on-premises==.
- The group ==!doesn't have to be role-assignable==. It must be role-assignable only if you assign an *Entra role* to it. Up to 500 role-assignable groups per tenant; more groups can be in PIM.
- Use a role-assignable group anyway for sensitive access. Ordinary groups can be changed by Groups Administrators, and helpdesk roles can reset members' passwords — a way around your approval step.
- Require approval for groups that elevate into Entra roles.
- For Exchange, SharePoint or Purview admin roles use PIM for Entra roles directly; group-based activation can take significant time to take effect there.

**5. Humans vs workloads (the AI angle).** PIM gates an *interactive session*. A **managed identity** or **service principal** gets tokens with no sign-in prompt, so it ==!can't complete an activation==. The training module's two-track rule:

- PIM governs the **humans** who configure AI services (for example eligible membership in a group holding Cognitive Services OpenAI Contributor).
- **Permanent, narrowly scoped RBAC** governs the **workload identities and agents** that run them (for example Storage Blob Data Contributor on one container).

**6. Access reviews and licensing.** An **access review** asks reviewers, on a schedule (one time, weekly, monthly, quarterly, semi-annually, annually), whether each assignment should stay. You can review eligible only, active only, or both; reviewers can be named users, the members themselves, or managers. **Auto apply results** removes denied users. **If reviewers don't respond** can be No change, Remove access, Approve access or Take recommendations (recommendations are based on sign-in in the last 30 days). Reviewing service principals needs ==@Microsoft Entra Workload ID Premium== on top.

PIM needs ==@Microsoft Entra ID P2 or Microsoft Entra ID Governance== licences for: users with eligible or time-bound assignments (roles or groups), approvers, users in an access review, and reviewers. If the licence expires: permanent Entra role assignments stay, ==!eligible Entra role assignments are removed==, ongoing access reviews end, PIM settings are deleted and PIM stops sending emails.

## Where it lives

Everything is in the **Microsoft Entra admin center** (`entra.microsoft.com`), not the Azure portal blade for the resource.

| Task | Path |
|---|---|
| Entra role assignments | `ID Governance > Privileged Identity Management > Microsoft Entra roles > Roles > {role} > Add assignments` |
| Entra role settings | `… > Microsoft Entra roles > Roles > {role} > Role settings > Edit` |
| Azure resource role assignment | `… > Azure resources > {subscription / RG / resource} > Manage > Roles > Add assignments` |
| Azure resource role settings | `… > Azure resources > {resource} > Settings > {role} > Edit` |
| PIM for Groups | `… > Groups > {group} > Assignments` (Member or Owner) |
| Activate your own role | `… > My roles > Eligible assignments > Activate` (tabs for Entra roles, Azure resources, Groups) |
| Approve requests | `… > Approve requests` |
| Access reviews | `… > Microsoft Entra roles` (or the subscription under Azure resources) `> Access reviews > New` |

Programmatically: the Microsoft Graph PIM APIs. Role settings are `unifiedRoleManagementPolicy` objects whose settings are called *rules*.

```http
GET https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0/policies/roleManagementPolicies?$filter=scopeId eq '/' and scopeType eq 'DirectoryRole'&$expand=rules
```

## Exam traps

- **"Make the managed identity eligible in PIM."** ==!Wrong.== Workload identities can't activate. Give them a permanent, narrowly scoped role assignment; put PIM on the humans who manage that assignment.
- **Approval with no approvers.** If every Global Administrator / Privileged Role Administrator is eligible-only, approval is required and no approvers are named, ==!you lock yourself out==. Keep at least two break-glass accounts with a *permanent active* Global Administrator assignment, excluded from PIM.
- **Break-glass in PIM?** ==+No.== The activation path depends on MFA, approvers and service health — the things that fail in an emergency.
- **Where are Azure resource role settings set?** ==+Per role, per scope== — not once per role tenant-wide.
- **Who sets Entra role settings?** At least ==+Privileged Role Administrator==. Security Administrator ==!can't==.
- **Who manages Azure resource assignments in PIM?** ==+Owner or User Access Administrator== on the resource — not an Entra role.
- **Ticket number** is ==!not validated==.
- **MFA on activation always prompts?** ==!No== — a user who already did strong auth may pass silently. Force a fresh challenge with an **authentication context** whose Conditional Access policy sets sign-in frequency to **Every time**.
- **PIM for Groups needs a role-assignable group?** ==!No==, only when an Entra role is assigned to the group.
- **Licence expiry** removes eligible assignments; it does ==!not== remove permanent ones.
- **Extend / renew** a time-bound assignment: the user requests it; a Global Administrator or Privileged Role Administrator approves.
- **PIM also protects you from yourself:** it blocks removing the **last active Global Administrator** and the **last active Privileged Role Administrator** assignment.
