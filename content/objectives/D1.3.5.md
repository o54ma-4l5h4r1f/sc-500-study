## Azure first

- A **security principal** is anything that can be granted access: a **user**, a **group**, a **service principal** (an app's identity) or a **managed identity** (an identity Azure runs for a resource).
- A **role definition** is a list of allowed operations, such as `Microsoft.Compute/virtualMachines/restart/action`.
- A **scope** is where the access applies: management group, subscription, resource group or single resource. Lower scopes inherit from higher ones.
- **Control plane** operations manage the resource (create a storage account). **Data plane** operations touch what is inside (read a blob). Roles list them separately.

See the Foundations pages listed above, especially *azure-rbac-and-entra-roles*.

## In one sentence

An Azure role assignment glues **who** (principal) + **what** (role definition) + **where** (scope); managing them well means picking the narrowest built-in role at the narrowest scope, assigning to groups, and constraining the few roles that can hand out access.

## Why it exists

Your SOC experience says it plainly: the incidents that hurt most start with an account that had far more access than it needed. Thirty-seven Owners on a production subscription means thirty-seven ways to delete it — and thirty-seven ways to grant an attacker more access. Built-in roles exist so you rarely need Owner or Contributor at all.

Access is **additive**: a Reader assignment at the subscription and a Contributor assignment at a resource group give Contributor in that group. ==!Azure RBAC alone cannot block inherited access== — only a **deny assignment** subtracts, and you cannot create those directly (Azure creates them, for example for managed applications and deployment stacks).

## What actually matters

**1. The five fundamental roles.**

| Role | Manage resources | Assign roles | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| ==@Owner== | yes, everything | yes | the widest blast radius |
| ==@Contributor== | yes, everything | ==!no== | also cannot write policy, manage Blueprints assignments, share image galleries, cancel subscriptions |
| ==@Reader== | view only | no | control-plane reads |
| ==@User Access Administrator== | no (reads only) | yes — `Microsoft.Authorization/*`, including policy | can assign itself Owner |
| ==@Role Based Access Control Administrator== | no (reads only) | yes — role assignments only, ==+not Azure Policy== | can assign itself Owner **unless you add a condition** |

All but Reader are **privileged administrator roles** (the portal lists them on a separate tab). Any role holding `*`, `*/write`, `*/delete`, `roleAssignments/write`, `roleDefinitions/write` or `denyAssignments/write` counts as privileged.

**2. Service-specific first.** Before Contributor, look for `<service> Contributor` or `<service> Reader`: **Network Contributor**, **Virtual Machine Contributor**, **Monitoring Reader**, **Backup Operator**. For data, look for **Data** in the name: **Storage Blob Data Reader** reads blobs without keys or settings; **Key Vault Secrets User** reads secret values without vault management. ==!Owner and Contributor have no DataActions== — they reach data only through keys or access policies.

**3. Conditions on role assignments.** A condition adds an extra check to one assignment.

- **Data-plane ABAC (Azure attribute-based access control):** for storage blobs and queues, for example "read only blobs whose index tag `Project` = `Cascade`" or only one container.
- **Delegation conditions:** on Owner, User Access Administrator or Role Based Access Control Administrator, limit **which roles** the delegate can assign, **to which principal types** (users, groups, service principals) and **to which principals**. Templates: *Constrain roles*, *Constrain roles and principal types*, *Constrain roles and principals*, *Allow all except specific roles* (exclude Owner, RBAC Administrator and UAA so the delegate cannot create more delegates).
- In the portal this is the **Conditions** tab: ==+"Allow user to only assign selected roles to selected principals (fewer privileges)"==. Microsoft recommends it for every privileged assignment.

**4. Groups and limits.** Assign roles to **Microsoft Entra security groups**; membership changes do not consume new assignments. Limits: **5,000** role assignments per subscription (subscription, resource-group and resource scopes) and **500** per management group, fixed. PIM **eligible** assignments do not count.

> **Contradiction:** the training unit states "up to 4,000 role assignments per subscription". Current product docs say **5,000**. Product docs win.

**5. Change safely.** Use **Check access** first to see direct, group-inherited and parent-scope assignments. **Add the narrower assignment before removing the broader one**, or you cause an outage. For automation, declare role assignments in Bicep/ARM (`Microsoft.Authorization/roleAssignments`) so they live in source control.

**6. Who can assign.** You need `Microsoft.Authorization/roleAssignments/write` at the scope: Owner, User Access Administrator or Role Based Access Control Administrator. ==!Contributor cannot assign roles.== A Global Administrator has **no** Azure access by default; flipping *Access management for Azure resources* grants them User Access Administrator at root scope (`/`).

## Where it lives

Azure portal > *any scope* > **Access control (IAM)**: tabs **Check access**, **Role assignments** (filter by type, role; chart of the count against the limit), **Roles** (search by service), **Deny assignments**. **+ Add > Add role assignment** walks **Role** (Job function roles / Privileged administrator roles) > **Members** (User, group, or service principal / **Managed identity**) > **Conditions** > **Assignment type** (Active / Eligible, with PIM) > **Review + assign**.

![Access control IAM page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/role-based-access-control/media/shared/rg-access-control.png){Access control (IAM) on a resource group: Check access, Role assignments, Roles, Deny assignments}

![Constrained condition option](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/role-based-access-control/media/shared/condition-constrained.png){Conditions tab on a privileged role: choose the constrained (fewer privileges) option}

```bash
az role assignment create --assignee <object-id-or-upn> \
  --role "Network Contributor" --scope /subscriptions/<sub>/resourceGroups/rg-network

# Managed identity: use its principal (object) ID
az role assignment create --assignee-object-id <mi-principal-id> --assignee-principal-type ServicePrincipal \
  --role "Key Vault Secrets User" --scope <key-vault-resource-id>

az role assignment list --scope /subscriptions/<sub> --role Owner --include-inherited
az role assignment delete --assignee <upn> --role Contributor --scope /subscriptions/<sub>
```

Conditions are passed with `--condition "<expression>" --condition-version "2.0"`.

## Exam traps

- "Let a team lead grant only Backup Reader/Backup Contributor to the Sales group" → **Role Based Access Control Administrator** with a **condition** (constrain roles and principals), not Owner or User Access Administrator.
- "Grant access management but not resource management" → UAA or RBAC Administrator. "Without the ability to change Azure Policy" → **RBAC Administrator**.
- "Contributor cannot assign a role / cannot create a policy" → by design (`Microsoft.Authorization/*/Write` is a NotAction).
- "Read blobs, no keys" → **Storage Blob Data Reader**, not Reader.
- "Engineer needs VMs in three resource groups" → three assignments at the RG scopes, not one at the subscription.
- "Remove access inherited from the subscription for one resource group" → impossible with role assignments; restructure scopes.
- "RoleAssignmentLimitExceeded" → move to group-based assignments (or PIM eligible).
- Global Administrator ≠ Azure Owner.

## Skip

- **Skip:** the 300+ built-in role catalogue. Know the five fundamentals, the privileged-role list and the naming pattern (`Contributor`/`Reader`/`Data`).
- **Skip:** the Copilot-in-Azure "help me pick a role" button in the portal walkthrough.
- **Skip:** ABAC condition syntax (`@Resource[...]`, `ForAnyOfAnyValues:GuidEquals`). Know what conditions *do* and the delegation templates by name.
- **Skip:** Azure RBAC data-residency notes and classic subscription administrator roles (Service Administrator, Co-Administrator); retired history.
- **Skip:** PIM eligible-assignment settings here; PIM is taught on D1.1.1.
