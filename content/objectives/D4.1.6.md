## Azure first

- A **resource** is anything you create in Azure inside a **resource group** and **region**. Defender EASM is deployed as its own resource, called a **workspace**.
- Your organisation's **internet-facing footprint** is made of public things: **domains** (contoso.com), **hosts** (www.contoso.com), public **IP addresses** and **IP blocks**, **ASNs** (the routing number a network owner announces), **SSL/TLS certificates**, and **WHOIS** registration records.
- Azure RBAC roles **Owner**, **Contributor** and **Reader** decide who can change or only view the workspace.

See the Foundations pages listed above. EASM needs no VNet, agent or connector — it looks at you from the internet.

## In one sentence

Defender EASM starts from a few assets you know are yours (**seeds**), follows public connections from them to find everything else that looks like yours on the internet, keeps that inventory current, and flags the risky parts — expired certificates, open admin ports, CVEs on exposed services.

## Why it exists

You can only protect what you know exists. Acquisitions, forgotten test sites and shadow IT leave internet-facing servers that no CMDB lists and no agent covers. Attackers scan the whole internet and find them anyway. EASM does the attacker's reconnaissance for you.

CSPM and MDVM look from the inside out at resources you already onboarded. EASM looks from the outside in, including assets you didn't know you had. Think of walking around your own building at night with a torch, rather than reading the floor plan.

## What actually matters

**1. It's an Azure resource with a trial.** Azure portal > search **Microsoft Defender EASM** > **Create** > subscription, resource group, name, region (from a supported list such as East US or West Europe). Creating it needs **Contributor**. **Owner/Contributor** can edit resources and inventory; **Reader** is view-only. ==!No cross-tenant access==, not even through Azure Lighthouse. A new EASM resource starts with a ==@30-day free trial==; after that you pay per **billable asset**.

**2. Discovery: prebuilt or custom.** **Getting Started** > search your organisation > **Build my Attack Surface** uses Microsoft's preconfigured attack surface. For more control, **Manage > Discovery > Add Discovery Group**: name, **recurring frequency** (default ==@Weekly==; **Never** = one run), seeds, optional **exclusions** (for example subsidiaries), then **Create & Run**. Seed types: organisation names, domains, IP blocks, hosts, email contacts, ASNs, WHOIS organisations. ==!Private IP addresses can't be seeds== — EASM is outside-in only. Organise groups by business unit, brand or acquisition.

**3. Asset states decide what counts.**

| State | Meaning | In dashboards? | Billable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| ==@Approved Inventory== | yours; you're responsible | ==+Yes== (scanned daily) | ==@Yes== |
| **Candidate** | linked to your seeds, ownership unconfirmed | No (scanned only during discovery) | No |
| **Dependency** | third party that directly supports your assets (CDN, hosting IP) | No | No |
| **Monitor Only** | related but not controlled (franchisees, sister companies) | No | No |
| **Requires Investigation** | low-confidence link; review manually | No | No |

Review **Candidates** and move what's yours to **Approved Inventory**, or the dashboards will under-report. Assets are also tagged **recent** (seen in the latest scans) or **historic** (seen before, not now — often a forgotten server still worth checking).

**4. What gets billed.** Only Approved Inventory: approved **host:IP combinations** (resolved in the last 30 days), approved **domains**, and approved active **IP addresses** (an open port or SSL certificate seen in the last 30 days). Duplicates are removed (a subdomain resolving to the same IP as its parent isn't counted twice). See **Manage > Billable assets**.

**5. Eight dashboards.** **Overview**, **Inventory changes** (added/removed, 7 or 30 days), **Attack surface summary** (asset types, high/medium/low findings, cloud hosting, sensitive services, SSL and domain expiry, IP reputation), **Security posture** (CVE exposure, domain administration, hosting, open ports, SSL configuration), **GDPR compliance**, **OWASP Top 10**, **CWE Top 25** and **CISA known exploits**. Dashboards show Approved assets only; every chart drills into a filtered inventory list and exports to CSV. CVEs that don't apply can be marked **non-applicable**. OWASP findings come from what's visible without authentication — ==!EASM isn't a penetration test==.

**6. Integrations.**

| Where | What you get | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| **Defender CSPM** (Defender for Cloud) | outside-in discovery of internet-facing cloud resources, ==@attack paths starting from internet-exposed IPs==, explorer queries correlating exposed IPs | ==+Included in Defender CSPM==, no EASM licence, no setup |
| **Microsoft Security Exposure Management** (Defender portal) | **External Attack Surface Protection** initiative; *pre-built footprint* (high-level only) or *full integration* with your EASM workspace (asset detail) | full integration needs an EASM workspace (trial or paid); configure with **Global Administrator** or **Core security settings (manage)** |
| **Data connections** | export Approved assets and attack-surface insights to **Log Analytics** (Sentinel) or **Azure Data Explorer** | target platform's pricing |

The full EASM workspace adds what the built-in CSPM integration doesn't: the eight dashboards, custom discovery groups and inventory management.

- **Azure portal** > Microsoft Defender EASM > *workspace*: General > **Getting Started**; Manage > **Discovery**, **Billable assets**, **Data connections**; **Inventory**; dashboards in the left menu.
- **Defender portal** > Exposure Management > Initiatives > **External Attack Surface Protection** > Open initiative page > Connect data source.
- **Defender portal** > Cloud security > Attack path analysis (filter **Internet exposed**) and Azure portal > Defender for Cloud > Cloud security explorer for the CSPM integration.

## Exam traps

- "Find internet-facing assets nobody inventoried, including from an acquisition" → ==+Defender EASM== discovery with new seeds. Not CSPM (only sees onboarded resources), not MDVM (only enrolled machines).
- "Assets found but missing from dashboards" → they're **Candidate**; approve them.
- "Reduce EASM cost" → only **Approved Inventory** is billed; move non-owned assets to Dependency / Monitor Only.
- "Attack paths starting from an internet-exposed IP, with no extra licence" → **Defender CSPM** built-in EASM integration.
- "Need custom discovery groups and the OWASP dashboard" → a full **EASM workspace**.
- "Add 10.0.0.0/8 as a seed" → ==!not allowed== (private range).
- "Manage a customer's EASM through Lighthouse" → not supported.
- "Send EASM data to Sentinel" → **Data connections** to Log Analytics.

## Skip

- **Skip:** the per-dashboard chart catalogue (domain EPP status codes, cookie posture, registrar patterns). Know the eight dashboard names and what Security posture and Attack surface summary cover.
- **Skip:** billing edge cases beyond "Approved only, host:IP / domain / IP, 30 days". Prices are reference material.
- **Skip:** data-connection plumbing (workspace keys, Azure Data Explorer user/ingestor permissions).
- **Skip:** data residency and retention figures (IP addresses kept 30 days, deletion within 180 days, 75-day retention after resource deletion).
- **Skip:** the EASM REST API and labels. Useful at work, not on the blueprint.
- **Skip:** unit 4's Contoso findings (two Telnet hosts, 14 certificates). Story, not facts.
