## Azure first

- **Microsoft Defender for Cloud** is the Azure service that checks how your cloud resources are configured (posture) and, with paid plans, protects workloads. You met its alerts as an analyst; here you use its *posture* side.
- A **security standard** in Defender for Cloud is an Azure Policy **initiative** — a bundle of policy definitions — that Defender for Cloud reads and scores.
- A **management group** sits above subscriptions. Assigning something there covers every subscription under it, including future ones.
- Defender for Cloud also connects **AWS accounts** and **GCP projects**, so one dashboard can score all three clouds.

See the Foundations pages listed above, especially *defender-for-cloud-basics*.

> **Two pages, one module.** This page is the **governance side**: assigning standards, reading the dashboard, tracing control gaps, producing reports. The posture-management view of the same dashboard — comparing frameworks and tracking compliance as part of overall posture — is on **D4.1.2 "Evaluate compliance against security frameworks"**. Read both; the facts are shared.

## In one sentence

The **Regulatory compliance** dashboard maps your resources' recommendation results onto the controls of frameworks such as ISO 27001, NIST SP 800-53 or PCI DSS, so you can see which controls pass, which fail, and which resource to fix.

## Why it exists

An auditor does not ask "is your secure score 72%?". They ask "show me control A.8.24 of ISO 27001". The dashboard translates. It is the same health check as the recommendations list, re-sorted into the auditor's chapter headings. The fix for a failing control is almost always a recommendation you already know.

## What actually matters

**1. Defaults and prerequisites.**

- When you enable Defender for Cloud, the ==@Microsoft cloud security benchmark (MCSB)== is assigned by default. AWS accounts get MCSB **plus** AWS Foundational Security Best Practices; GCP projects get MCSB **plus** GCP Default. MCSB v2 is in preview alongside the current MCSB.
- Adding any **non-default** standard needs at least one paid Defender plan — ==!any plan except Defender for Servers Plan 1 or Defender for APIs Plan 1==. Foundational CSPM alone is not enough.
- The built-in list is long: NIST SP 800-53 R5.1.1, NIST CSF v2.0, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, PCI DSS v4.0.1, CIS Azure Foundations, SOC 2, HIPAA, FedRAMP H & M, NIS2, GDPR, DORA, EU AI Act, and more.

**Why "standard = initiative" matters.** Turning a standard on creates an Azure Policy initiative assignment at that scope. That is why the permissions are policy permissions, why exclusions and exemptions behave as in D1.3.1, and why a management-group assignment flows down to every subscription beneath it. The dashboard is a scoring view on top of policy results — no separate engine.

**2. Assign where it aggregates.** Assign at the **highest applicable scope** (management group, AWS management account, GCP organization). Children inherit, and the dashboard rolls the results up. ==!If no in-scope resource is relevant, an assigned standard does not appear on the dashboard at all== — that is not a failure.

**3. Read the three states correctly.** Green = all in-scope resources pass. Red = at least one fails. **Grey** = the control cannot be assessed automatically: a process control (training, policy documents), a Microsoft responsibility under shared responsibility (datacenter security), or one with no automated check yet. Grey is ==!not== missing coverage. Close it with **manual attestation**: select the assessment under *Manual attestation and evidence*, choose subscriptions, **Attest**, attach evidence.

A control that fails on one storage account fails for the whole standard, and the same recommendation can fail controls in several standards at once. Fix the recommendation once and every mapped control improves together. That is the practical way to prioritise: filter Recommendations by the framework you are audited on, then work highest-severity first.

**4. Drill from control to resource.** Expand a control → select a failing assessment → the recommendation page lists affected resources and the fix. **Control details** has three tabs: **Overview** (what the control means), **Your Actions** (automated and manual assessments you own), **Microsoft Actions** (what Microsoft does for you). Assessments run approximately every **12 hours**, so a fix shows up after the next run.

**5. Produce evidence.**

| Need | Feature |
|---|---|
| Point-in-time evidence for an auditor | **Download report** — PDF summary or CSV with resource-level data |
| Microsoft's own certificates (ISO, SOC, PCI attestations of Azure itself) | **Audit reports** |
| Trend over time | **Compliance over time** workbook |
| Feed a SIEM or another tool | **Continuous export** to Log Analytics or Event Hubs — continuous stream or weekly snapshot |
| Act when a control flips | **Workflow automation** triggers a Logic App on a regulatory compliance assessment change |
| One view across Microsoft 365 and cloud | **Microsoft Purview Compliance Manager** picks up the data automatically once a standard is assigned |

**6. Who can do what.** Adding a standard: ==@Owner== or Policy Contributor on the subscription (the docs' name; the built-in Azure role is **Resource Policy Contributor**). Managing standards with the full dashboard: at minimum **Resource Policy Contributor + Security Admin**. Viewing compliance data: ==+Reader== on the subscription works; ==!Security Reader does not== see policy compliance data.

## Exam traps

- "Auditor needs ISO 27001 evidence" → assign the ISO standard, then **Download report**. "Auditor wants Microsoft's own ISO certificate" → **Audit reports**.
- "Cannot add PCI DSS" → no qualifying paid plan (Defender for Servers **Plan 1** alone does not qualify).
- "Standard assigned but not on the dashboard" → no relevant resources in scope.
- "Grey control" → manual attestation, not a missing plan.
- "User has Security Reader but sees no compliance data" → give **Reader**.
- "Assign once for 40 subscriptions" → management group.
- "Change a standard from the Defender portal" → cannot; configuration is in the Azure portal.
- MCSB is the default baseline for Azure, AWS and GCP — the AWS/GCP extras are *in addition*, not instead.
