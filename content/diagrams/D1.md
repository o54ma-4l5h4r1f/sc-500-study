# D1 — how it all connects

Domain 1 has three halves of one story. **Entra ID** decides *who* gets a token. **Key Vault** holds the secrets and keys those identities use. **Governance** (Policy, locks, RBAC, Defender for Cloud, Backup) decides *what anyone may change*. The objective pages teach each piece alone. These seven pictures show how the pieces join.

Dashed, muted boxes are **forward-links**: a construct from another domain that looks like a D1 thing or sits next to it. ==@dashed = another domain==. They are pointers only; the detail lives in that domain.

## 1 · From identity to resource — the token path

Every access request in Azure walks this path. A **principal** (human, app or managed identity) proves who it is to Entra ID. Entra ID runs the checks that apply to *that kind* of principal, then issues a token. The token reaches a resource, where a **role on the target** decides what it opens. Note the short-cut on the managed-identity row: it never meets Conditional Access or PIM, which is why narrow RBAC and access reviews are its only controls.

## 2 · Key Vault — will this request succeed?

The decision tree for "why did I get 403?". First ask **which plane** the call is for. Control-plane calls never meet the vault firewall. Data-plane calls must pass **two independent gates**: the network (firewall, private endpoint, trusted-services bypass) and the permission model (an RBAC data role, or a legacy access-policy entry). The right role cannot get past a closed firewall, and an open network grants nothing. D1.2.3 and D1.2.4 each draw one gate; this picture puts them in sequence.

## 3 · A secret's life — from leak to rotation

This is the end-to-end story the D1.2 pages tell one piece at a time. Defender CSPM **finds** a plaintext secret. You **store** it in a vault that cannot lose it. You **grant** one managed identity the narrowest data role. The app **uses** a versionless reference. A rotation policy or Event Grid **rotates** it. AuditEvent and Defender for Key Vault **watch** who reads it — and a suspicious read sends you back to rotate again.

## 4 · One request, every governance gate

Whoever sends it — portal, CLI, Bicep or a pipeline — a change to an Azure resource meets the same gates at Azure Resource Manager. **Any one gate can stop it**, and more rights at one gate never override another (an Owner still hits a lock or a Policy deny). After the resource exists, Policy keeps scoring it, and Defender for Cloud turns those scores into standards and recommendations. The order of effects *inside* Azure Policy is drawn on [D1.3.1](../objective/D1.3.1.html); the pipeline layer is drawn on [D1.3.9](../objective/D1.3.9.html). This picture places them in one line.

## 6 · Where a role can sit — Azure scopes versus Entra scopes

## 7 · Backup protection layers

Each layer below takes one move away. **Soft delete** keeps deleted data recoverable. **Immutability** stops early deletion and retention cuts. **MUA with a Resource Guard** makes a second person approve the risky operations — including turning the first two off.
